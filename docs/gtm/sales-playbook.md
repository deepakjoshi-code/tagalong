## 1. Read this first: the arithmetic that governs every deal

The 1‑pack lands in our warehouse at **$17.20** and retails at **$29.99**. Everything a partner asks for comes out of $12.79.

| Structure the partner will ask for | What we net | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Standard specialty wholesale, **50% of MSRP** = $14.99 | **−$3.66 per unit** | **No.** Every unit is a loss. This is the ask you will hear most often |
| Mass‑retail wholesale, 50% plus co‑op and allowances | worse than −$5 | **No** |
| Consignment at 40% commission = $17.99 | −$0.66 | **No** — 35% ($19.49, +$0.84) is the most we can take |
| **Break‑even floor** = $18.65 (landed $17.20 + $0.60 freight out + $0.85 defect reserve) | $0.00 | The physical limit. 38% off MSRP |
| **Our minimum acceptable wholesale: $20.99 (30% off MSRP)** | **+$2.34 (7.8%)** | **Yes — this is the number you quote** |
| Program/bulk purchase, 10+ units, single shipment | $2.00–4.10 per tag | **Yes — the best structure we have** |
| Affiliate / referral, no inventory on either side | **+$4.10 per unit** after a $4.00 bounty, because the sale lands on DTC | **Yes — the default** |

**Three consequences, and they are not negotiable at v1 unit costs.**

1. **We are a direct‑and‑marketplace brand.** Wholesale is a marketing expense with a cap, not a revenue line. Budget it as such and report it as such.
2. **The only offers we lead with are affiliate and bulk.** Both avoid a retail margin entirely. A partner who must have shelf margin gets 30% off, never 50%, and hears why in one honest sentence.
3. **A BOM reduction changes this conversation and nothing else will.** A 50% keystone with a real margin needs a landed cost near $12, which needs a BOM near $8. That is not in this hardware generation. Do not promise a partner that "the price improves at volume" beyond the 10% we can evidence at 50k units.

**How to say no to 50%, without losing the partner.** *"I can't do fifty and stay in business — this is a $12 bill of materials in a $30 box, and I'd rather tell you that than take the order and disappear in a year. What I can do is give you the product with no inventory risk at all, and pay you on every unit your customers buy."* Then offer the affiliate structure. It converts better than the discount would have.

## 3. Channel 1 — Paediatric dentists and dental hygienists

### 3.1 Why they say yes

Every paediatric practice is asked the same question twenty times a week: *"how do I get them to brush for two minutes?"* Today they answer with a sticker chart, a sand timer, or an app that puts a phone screen in a bathroom with a wet‑handed six‑year‑old.

Tagalong's toothbrush pack celebrates a **cumulative 120 seconds** of actual brushing motion, says nothing if the brush is knocked about in a drawer, and offers rather than scolds when a session ends early. There is no screen, no account and no data, so a practice can recommend it without adding a line to its own privacy assessment.

### 3.2 The offer, in the order we make it

| # | Offer | Terms | What it needs from them |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Practice Partner (affiliate)** — the default | We send two free tags for the surgery and a box of 250 cards with the practice's own code. **$4.00 per redeemed unit**, paid quarterly. Zero inventory, zero risk | A card in the goodie bag and a tag on the counter |
| 2 | **Practice Pack** — patient rewards and internal use | **10 tags, 5 chargers, mounts, $199** (equivalent to $19.90 a tag, 34% off) | A purchase order. MOQ 10 |
| 3 | **Retail shelf** — practices that already sell brushes | 6 or 12 units at **30% off MSRP**, MAP‑bound, defective‑only returns | Shelf space and the shelf card |
| 4 | **Group / DSO agreement** — 25+ practices | 30% off, one invoice, co‑branded cards, a named account manager | A signed master agreement |

### 3.3 The pitch (90 seconds, then hand them the phone)

> "You get asked how to make two minutes of brushing happen. The answer people are selling right now is a phone in the bathroom.
>
> This is a waterproof tag that clips to the base of the brush. It celebrates the two minutes — the real two minutes, measured on the brush, not on a timer the child can walk away from. If they stop at forty seconds it offers, once, and then it's quiet.
>
> There's no microphone, no camera and no account, and nothing leaves the parent's phone — so recommending it doesn't put anything on your plate.
>
> Here, set the age to one of your patients and tap Brush."

### 3.4 Guardrails

- **No clinical or health claims, by us or by them.** It celebrates the timer. It does not improve brushing outcomes, prevent decay, or treat anything. This is written into the partner agreement and into every asset we supply.
- Practices may not modify our copy. They get approved cards, a shelf card and a poster; anything else goes through us.
- Never position it as a replacement for supervision or for professional advice.
- No inducement structures that a dental board could read as a kickback: the affiliate bounty is disclosed on the card, and Practice Packs are sold, not gifted, above a nominal quantity.

### 3.5 Pipeline target

40 practices approached in wave 1 via state paediatric dental associations and two DSO groups · **8 signed Practice Partners and 2 Practice Packs by end Q4 2027** · one DSO conversation open.

## 4. Channel 2 — Schools, PTAs and therapy settings

Three buyers live behind the word "school", and only two of them can buy.

### 4.1 PTA and school fundraising — the one that works

PTAs accept a 30% margin because they are raising funds, not running a shop. They buy a carton, sell at MSRP at a fair or an online store, and keep $9 a unit.

| | |
|---|---|
| Offer | 12, 24 or 48 units at **30% off MSRP**, prepaid, MAP‑bound |
| Support | A demo phone stand for the table, a shelf card, an A5 parent leaflet, and a **QR to the demo** so a parent can hear it in the queue |
| Why it is worth more than the margin | It is parent‑to‑parent acquisition. One table at a summer fair reaches 200 families with a live demo, at a customer acquisition cost of zero |
| Guardrail | No fundraising claims we cannot support, no percentage‑to‑charity claims on our side, and no use of children's images in the materials |

### 4.2 Occupational therapy and special education

Routine transitions, brushing and hydration cues are a real fit, and the tag lives on the child's own object rather than on a shared device. The school quiet window — a separate window with its own weekday mask, stored on the tag — is what makes a talking object acceptable in a classroom at all.

| | |
|---|---|
| Offer | **Classroom Set: 10 tags, 5 chargers, 10 bottle straps, 10 zipper loops, a setup guide with the school window pre‑configured — $199** |
| Buyer | OT clinics, SEN coordinators, early‑intervention programmes, paediatric therapy practices |
| Guardrail | **No therapeutic, clinical or developmental claims.** It is a toy that speaks when something happens. Practitioners may decide it is useful; we do not tell them it is effective, and we never use the words *therapy*, *treatment*, *intervention* or *outcome* in our own materials |
| Privacy answer for a purchasing review | One page: no account, no cloud, no microphone, no camera, no location, no analytics, nothing transmitted to us. **There is no student data, so there is nothing to sign a data agreement about.** This is the strongest privacy answer in the category and it should be pre‑written, not improvised in a meeting |

### 4.3 District and local‑authority purchasing — deferred

Deliberately not pursued at v1. Procurement cycles run 3–9 months, our price point sits below most purchasing thresholds (which paradoxically makes it *harder* to buy, not easier), and there is no budget category for it. Revisit in Y2 with the one‑page privacy answer and an OT case study, and only in districts where a therapy budget already exists.

### 4.4 Pipeline target

20 PTAs approached, **6 orders** by Q4 2027 · 15 OT and SEN settings approached, **4 Classroom Sets** by Q1 2028.

## 5. Channel 3 — Family subscription boxes

### 5.1 The proposition to them, not to us

A subscription box does not need another filler item. It needs **one hero a year** that makes a non‑subscriber sign up, and that hero has to be filmable in eight seconds, explainable in one sentence, and safe to put in front of a parent audience.

What we bring: a $29.99 perceived‑value item, a reaction that films itself, a privacy story their audience is already asking about, no batteries to explain, no app for the child, and a demo their creators can use before the box even ships.

### 5.2 Two structures, in order of preference

| | **A · Insert + code** *(preferred)* | **B · Hero placement** |
|---|---|---|
| How it works | Their box carries a printed card with a partner code at a subsidised price; we fulfil and keep the customer relationship | They buy a box‑specific SKU in bulk and include the physical product |
| Their cost | Zero | **$21.00 per unit** (70% of MSRP), MOQ 500 |
| Our cost | A **$4–6 bounty** per redemption | — |
| Our contribution | $2.10–4.10 per redeemed unit, on a DTC sale | **$4.10** per unit (box SKU lands at $16.05 + $0.85 reserve) |
| Box SKU contents | — | Tag, bottle strap, charging puck, quick‑start and privacy cards, **paper wrap instead of the rigid box** — no lace clip, no adhesive base |
| Risk | Redemption rates can disappoint (plan 2–6%) | Inventory committed against one partner's forecast |
| Why we prefer A | We keep the customer, the email address and the review, and we carry no inventory | Reach, immediacy and a real unboxing moment |

**The honest limit:** many boxes buy at 40–60% off MSRP. At $12–18 a unit we lose money, and we say so in the first call rather than the fourth. If a box cannot reach $21.00, the answer is structure A — and structure A is a better deal for us anyway.

### 5.3 Guardrails

- We approve **all** creative. A box describing it as a tracker, a finder or a "smart" device is a breach.
- No resale of overstock on Amazon, eBay or any marketplace. This protects MAP and our Brand Registry position, and it is a termination clause.
- No box may bundle it with a product that contradicts the positioning — no screens, no location devices, no branded food.
- Android‑first must appear on the insert card until the iPhone app ships.

### 5.4 Pipeline target

12 boxes approached across the US and UK · **2 pilots signed by Q1 2028**, at least one on structure A.

## 7. The partner pitch

### 7.1 Run the meeting with the demo, not with a deck

The demo playground is the pitch. It works on any phone, needs no internet and no hardware, and it does something no slide can: it makes the buyer laugh in the first fifteen seconds.

**The sequence.** Open the demo → ask how old their own kids are → set that age band → hand them the phone → "tap Drop" → stay quiet. Then talk about terms.

### 7.2 The five‑minute structure

| Beat | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · The hook | 20 s | "It's a talking tag for kids' things. Strap it to a bottle and the bottle becomes a character." |
| 2 · **The demo in their hands** | 60 s | Their child's age, their choice of personality, three events |
| 3 · Why it sells | 60 s | $29.99 in the fastest‑growing US toy price tier ($30–69.99, +18% in 2025); no subscription; the privacy story their customers are already asking about |
| 4 · The one they will ask about | 30 s | "It isn't a finder and never will be. That's deliberate." |
| 5 · The offer | 60 s | Affiliate or bulk first. Wholesale only if they need shelf margin, and then 30% |
| 6 · The close | 30 s | One named next step with a date. Never "I'll send some information" |

### 7.3 One‑page leave‑behind

Front: the product photograph, "Give anything a voice.", "It talks. It doesn't track.", four sample lines, the six privacy nos, the price ladder.
Back: what's in the box, the mount system, terms (§9), lead time, certifications, support and returns, contact, a QR to the demo.

### 7.4 What to bring, and what not to say

**Bring:** a charged phone with the demo, a DVT tag from March 2027, a shelf card, an empty retail box, the one‑pager.
**Never say:** 50% is possible · a ship date more precise than a month before G2 · any Amber claim (battery weeks, IP67, drop counts, age grade) · anything about finding, locating or tracking · any health, dental or developmental benefit · that content updates are automatic before v1.1 ships.

### 7.5 Partner objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "I need fifty points." | "I can't do fifty and still be here next year — it's a $12 bill of materials in a $30 box. Here's a structure with no inventory risk that pays you on every unit." |
| "Nobody knows this brand." | "That's why the first order is consignment. You carry no risk, and the demo sells it off the counter — hand it to a customer and they laugh." |
| "Is it a tracker? My customers will ask." | "No, and that's on the box: *It talks. It doesn't track.* No GPS, no finding network. It's deliberate — a device that can find a child can be used against one." |
| "Talking toys get returned." | "Volume is capped in the hardware and it has two quiet windows, including school hours. And we carry the return risk: sixty days, we pay the postage." |
| "What happens when you run out of money?" | "Nothing happens to the product. There's no server to switch off and no subscription to cancel. That's the whole architecture, and it's published." |
| "Can I sell it on Amazon too?" | "No — and that protects your margin. We hold MAP across every channel including our own, and marketplace resale is a termination clause." |
| "Will you support us with advertising money?" | "No co‑op funds at this price. What you get instead is the demo, the shelf card, a counter unit, and creator content you can re‑post." |

## 8. Compliance guardrails for every B2B2C conversation

These are not suggestions. A children's product sold through a health or education setting attracts a different class of scrutiny than a toy shop.

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No clinical, dental, therapeutic or developmental claims**, by us or by a partner using our materials | Unsubstantiated health claims are actionable in every launch market, and the content guidelines already ban health framing to children |
| 2 | **No child's image without written parental consent**, and none in paid media before launch | Ours to enforce even when a partner supplies the photograph |
| 3 | **Affiliate bounties are disclosed** on the card and in the partner agreement | FTC Endorsement Guides, ASA CAP, ACCC. A dentist recommending a product for a fee must say so |
| 4 | **The privacy answer is one page and pre‑written**, and it never over‑claims: "the tag and the app collect nothing" — not "we have no analytics anywhere" | The website and store are ordinary web properties; the distinction must be in writing before a school asks |
| 5 | **No partner may claim it finds, locates or monitors anything** | It is false, and it invites tracker‑misuse scrutiny |
| 6 | **Amber claims are blocked in partner materials too** | A partner's flyer is our advertising |
| 7 | **MAP applies to partners and to us equally** | The moment we break it, it is unenforceable |
| 8 | **No marketplace resale by partners** | Protects MAP, Brand Registry and the buy box |

## 9. Wholesale terms sheet

Standard terms for specialty, PTA, practice and experience‑retail accounts. Any deviation needs Finance sign‑off against §1.

| Term | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Wholesale price** | **30% off MSRP.** 1‑pack $20.99 · 2‑pack $34.99 · Family 4‑pack $69.99 · Mount kit $5.59 · Mount 3‑pack $13.99 · Spare charger $10.49 |
| **Consignment alternative** | 35% commission on the retail price, monthly settlement, stock ours until sold, we pay inbound and recall freight |
| **Program / bulk** | Classroom Set (10 tags) $199 · Practice Pack (10 tags) $199 · Box SKU $21.00 at MOQ 500 |
| **MOQ** | Opening order 12 units (one carton), mixed colours available. Reorder 12 units |
| **Payment** | First order prepaid. **Net 30** thereafter on approved credit, with a $2,500 limit for the first six months |
| **Freight** | FOB our US warehouse. Free freight on orders of $1,500 or more; below that, partner's carrier or our rate |
| **Lead time** | 10 business days from PO in stock season; 4 weeks in Q4. Allocation applies in Q4 and is communicated in writing, not discovered |
| **MAP** | MSRP, with a 15% promotional floor inside the four named windows in `pricing-and-packaging.md` §5.3, maximum 14 days each. First breach: written notice. Second: supply suspended |
| **Channel restrictions** | No resale on Amazon, eBay, Walmart Marketplace, Etsy or any marketplace. No export outside the agreed territory. No unauthorised sub‑distribution |
| **Exclusivity** | None granted, in any territory or channel, at v1 |
| **Returns** | **Defective only**, within 12 months, RMA number required, credit not cash. **No stock balancing and no returns for slow sell‑through in year 1** |
| **Warranty pass‑through** | End consumers claim direct with us. Partners never carry the warranty burden, and never handle a battery return — the take‑back address is printed in the box |
| **Damage in transit** | Reported within 5 business days with photographs; we replace |
| **Marketing support** | Demo counter unit, shelf card, A5 parent leaflet, product photography, creator content to re‑post, listing copy. **No co‑op funds, no advertising allowances, no chargebacks accepted** |
| **Data** | We ask for sell‑through by SKU by month. We do not ask for, and will not accept, end‑customer personal data |
| **EDI / vendor portals** | Not supported at v1. PO by email, invoice by email |
| **Drop‑ship** | Not offered at v1 |
| **Term** | 12 months, rolling, 30 days' notice either side |
| **Territory at launch** | US, UK, CA, AU only. India, EU and the rest follow the certification and language schedule |

**Price list discipline.** One published wholesale list, one MSRP, no bespoke pricing. The first bespoke deal creates the precedent that ends the price list, and at a 7.8% wholesale contribution there is nothing to give away.
